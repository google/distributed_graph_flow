This document records the simulation and enumeration studies that validate the coverage guarantees, refutation checks, and baseline comparisons reported in [README.md](README.md), [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), [THEORY.md](THEORY.md), and [THEORY_SPACETIME.md](THEORY_SPACETIME.md). ## 1. Overview and Coverage Contract

A **miss** is the event $\theta \notin [L, U]$ for the true value $\theta$.
When the declared assumptions hold (the moment bound $M$, and on dependent
routes the dependence bound $\kappa$), the interval misses with probability at
most $\alpha = 1 - \text{level}$ (README §7, "Coverage guarantee"; Claims 1,
1′, 3, 3′ in [THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md](THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md) and Claims 4, 5, 7
in [THEORY.md](THEORY.md)):

$$\Pr(\text{miss}) \le \alpha, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \Pr(\text{miss} \wedge \texttt{UNREFUTED}) \le \alpha.$$

The library does **not** guarantee $\Pr(\text{miss} \mid \texttt{UNREFUTED})
\le \alpha$: among unrefuted results the miss rate can reach
$\alpha / \Pr(\texttt{UNREFUTED})$. Every coverage table below therefore uses
all Monte Carlo repetitions as the denominator and reports:

1. **Misses (all statuses):** repetitions with $\theta \notin [L, U]$,
   whatever the status.
2. **Joint (`miss and UNREFUTED`):** repetitions that missed and were
   `UNREFUTED` (on dependent routes: not refuted).
3. **`ASSUMPTION_REQUIRED`:** repetitions with no interval, because the
   effective sample size is below the minimum for $(M, \text{level})$ or the
   input is invalid.
4. **`REFUTED` / `TAIL_UNRESOLVED`:** repetitions where a check contradicted
   the declaration.

A conditional rate, where shown, is labelled as conditional.

## 2. Independent-Sample Coverage and Exact Hypergeometric Enumeration

### 2.1 Independent-Sample Coverage Study

This study evaluates the independent-sample estimator (`independent.interval.confidence_interval`, `independent.sketch.from_data`, `independent.sketch.from_array`, `independent.sketch.merge`, and `independent.jax_accumulator.init` / `update` / `all_reduce` / `to_sketch` / `init_r2` / `update_r2` / `all_reduce_r2` / `to_r2_sketch`) across continuous losses (`mae`, `mse`, `rmse`), binary/multiclass classification `accuracy` (evaluated via Clopper-Pearson at $\tilde\alpha = \alpha - \alpha^2/4$, Claim 3 of [THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md](THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md)), paired coefficient of determination (`r2`, §6 of [THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md](THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md)), user-declared effective sample size (`effective_n`) under equicorrelated Gaussian clusters (Claim 1′ of [THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md](THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md)), finite-pool without-replacement sampling (Claim 1″ of [THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md](THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md)), invalid inputs, and numerical agreement across all execution modes (`data`, `sketch_np`, `merged`, `sketch_jax`).

Includes 7 in-contract cells ($M_{\text{pool}} \le M_{\text{declared}}$) and 1 out-of-contract cell (`cell_id=92`, `mse`, $M_{\text{pool}} = 21.62 > M_{\text{declared}} = 16.0$).
  - **Block F — Invalid inputs (6 configurations, 19 cell-mode pairs, 200 repetitions each)**: $n=1{,}000, \alpha=0.05$ with single-element fault injection at a random index (`nan_residual` on `mae`, `inf_residual` on `mse`, `nan_prediction` on `accuracy`, `nan_label` on `r2`, `negative_summand` on `raw_summands`, and `indicator_two` on `accuracy_1d`).

### 2.2 Exact Hypergeometric Clopper-Pearson Enumeration

This deterministic calculation verifies Claim 3′ (`§4.2`, lines 783–828) of [THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md](THEORY_INDEPENDENT.md): for a without-replacement sample $K \sim \text{Hypergeom}(N, D, n)$ of size $1 \le n \le N$ from a finite pool of size $N$ with $0 \le D \le N$ successes ($\theta = D/N$), the Clopper-Pearson interval $[L_n(K), U_n(K)]$ evaluated at adjusted level $\tilde\alpha = \alpha - \alpha^2/4$ satisfies $\Pr_{\text{wor}}(\theta < L_n(K)) \le \alpha/2$ and $\Pr_{\text{wor}}(\theta > U_n(K)) \le \alpha/2$ for every $\alpha \in (0, 2-\sqrt{2}]$ (proved in Claim 3′ via the Vatutin-Mikhailov real-rooted PGF / Hoeffding Poisson-binomial tail bound for $k_0 \ge n\theta + 1$, the Greenberg-Mohri binomial median bound for $n\theta > 1$, and Markov's inequality plus the alternating-series bound for $n\theta \le 1, k_0 = 1$).

#### Setup

- **Grid**: 224 $(N, n, \alpha)$ cells formed by pool sizes $N \in \{10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000\}$, all sample sizes $n \in \{1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500\} \cap [1, N]$ together with $N-1$ and $N$, and nominal levels $\alpha \in \{0.10, 0.05, 0.01, 0.001\}$.
- **Method**: Exact summation of hypergeometric point probabilities $\Pr_{\text{Hypergeom}(N, D, n)}(K = k)$ over all $k \in \{0, \dots, n\}$ and all $D \in \{0, \dots, N\}$ (76,704 $(N, n, \alpha, D)$ states in total; no Monte Carlo sampling).
- **Seed**: Deterministic enumeration (no random number generator).

## 3. Temporal and Graph Coverage, Refutation Checks, and Partitioner Bounds

### 3.1 Temporal and Graph Coverage and Refutation Study

This study evaluates the dependent-data interval estimators (`temporal.temporal_interval`, `temporal.temporal_interval_from_shard`, `graph.graph_interval`, `graph.graph_interval_from_shard`, `graph.cluster_adjacency`, `partitioners.partition_graph`) and their refutation diagnostics (`refutation.check_uncorrelated`, `refutation.check_kappa`, `refutation.check_uncorrelated_edges`, `refutation.check_kappa_batches`, and the split-window drift warning, Proposition D of [THEORY.md](THEORY.md)) across temporal AR(1) fields, Poisson time-bucketed arrivals, adversarial unequal window counts, variance-shift drift, synthetic 20,000-node graphs, and invalid inputs.

#### Setup

- **Configurations**: 136 cells in six blocks (5,000 repetitions per cell in `T1`, `T2`, `T2b`, `T3`, `G1`; 200 in `F`). The randomized cells are drawn once from the seed; a draw that the library would refuse at the design stage is redrawn (up to 100 attempts). Shared randomized declarations: $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.01\}$ (probabilities 0.7 / 0.3), `m_mode` $\in \{$`default`, `tight`$\}$ (`tight` declares the true cluster-level $M_c$), `kappa_mode` $\in \{$`exact`, `one`, `half`$\}$ (probabilities 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2), and a residual scale log-uniform on $[10^{-3}, 10^{3}]$.
  1. **Temporal AR(1) fields (`T1`, 56 cells, `cell_id` 0–55, modes `t_data`, `t_shard`, `t_merged`)**: Gaussian AR(1) residuals with $\phi = 1 - 1/L$ ($\phi = 0$ for $L = 1$), split into $G$ equal windows.
     - Cells 0–3 (thin corner): $n = 20{,}000$, $L = 1{,}000$, $G = 100$, `mse`, $\alpha = 0.05$, $\text{VIF}_{\text{true}} = 5.544$, true $M_c = 2.758$; `kappa_mode` $\in \{$`exact`, `one`$\}$ × `m_mode` $\in \{$`default`, `tight`$\}$.
     - Cells 4–7 (high accuracy): $n = 20{,}000$, $G = 200$, `accuracy` with $p \in \{0.99, 0.999\}$, $L \in \{1, 4\}$, $\alpha = 0.05$, $\kappa = 1$, default $M$.
     - Cells 8–55 (randomized): $n \in \{2{,}000, 5{,}000, 20{,}000, 50{,}000\}$, $L \in \{1, 10, 100, 1{,}000\}$, $G \in \{50, 100, 200, 500\}$ with $G \le n/10$, metric $\in \{$`mse`, `rmse`, `mae`, `accuracy`$\}$ (accuracy $p \sim U(0.6, 0.999)$).
  2. **Poisson time-bucketed counts (`T2`, 12 cells, `cell_id` 56–67, modes `t_shard`, `t_merged`)**: inhomogeneous Poisson arrivals on $[0, 1]$ with expected $n = 20{,}000$ and linear intensity calibrated to a target count-heterogeneity $h \in \{0, 1.05, 1.2\}$, $G = 100$ equal-width buckets, AR(1) residuals with $L \in \{1, 10\}$, `mse`. With the default $M$, solving $D > 0$ for these designs gives thresholds of about $G \approx 2{,}600$–$4{,}000$ at $\alpha = 0.05$ (depending on cluster-size spread) and about $G \approx 21{,}000$ at $\alpha = 0.01$; below them the upper end is clipped. With $M = 3$, $G = 2{,}500$ already gives median intervals of width about 0.34.

A normal interval built from the true long-run variance of $e^2$, $2(1+\rho^2)/(1-\rho^2)$, would have relative width 0.023, 0.037 and 0.077 for the three values of $\rho$. - **Cantelli versus normal.** The library uses a one-sided Cantelli bound at $\alpha/2$ in place of a normal quantile: $\sqrt{(1-\alpha/2)/(\alpha/2)} = 6.24$ against $z_{0.975} = 1.96$, a factor of 3.2.
- **Item-level to window-level bound.** Lemma I′ converts the item-level $M$ to the window bound $M_c$ in a way that must hold even if the 25 items of a window were perfectly correlated, so here $M_c = M = 4$. The half-width scales with $\sqrt{M_c - 1}$, which contributes $\sqrt{3/0.13} = 4.8$, $\sqrt{3/0.33} = 3.0$ and $\sqrt{3/1.00} = 1.7$.

## 6. Range-Envelope Coverage

This study evaluates range-envelope intervals (`range_envelope.RangeDeclaration`, `range_envelope.range_candidates`, `range_envelope.range_kappa_fn` with `method="kronecker"`, `spacetime.choose_partition`, `cluster_sketch.PartialClusterShard`, and `cluster_bound.cluster_interval`; Claims RE0 and RE1 and Proposition RE2 of [THEORY_RANGE_ENVELOPE.md](THEORY_RANGE_ENVELOPE.md)) on synthetic space-time forecasting panels with an exact analytical target.

#### Setup

- **Field (`generate_moving_average_noise` and `generate_space_time_field` in `benchmark/range_generators.py`)**: $\eta_{u,t}$ is a unit-variance spatio-temporal moving average of i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ innovations over spatial hop radius $r_s = 1$ (`r_s_noise=1`, true pairwise spatial data range $R_s = 2 r_s = 2$ hops) and causal temporal lag depth $r_t = 2$ (`r_t_noise=2`, true pairwise temporal data range $R_t = r_t = 2$ steps). Three field variants are evaluated:
  - **Variant 1 (pure finite-range)**: $X_{u,t} = \eta_{u,t}$.
  - **Variant 2 (common per-time spatial shock)**: $X_{u,t} = \sqrt{1-\rho_0}\,\eta_{u,t} + \sqrt{\rho_0}\,Z_t$ with $Z_t \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ shared across all nodes at time $t$.
  - **Variant 3 (diffuse global term)**: $X_{u,t} = \sqrt{1-c/N_s}\,\eta_{u,t} + \sqrt{c/N_s}\,W_t$ with $W_t \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ shared across all nodes at time $t$.
- **Forecast and loss (`run_single_config_benchmark` and `compute_exact_theta` in `benchmark/range_coverage.py`)**: At each evaluated origin time $t \in \{L - 1, \dots, T - H - 1\}$, the prediction $\hat{Y}_{u,t+H}$ for $Y_{u,t+H} = X_{u,t+H}$ is the mean of $X$ over the $K = 1$-hop ball around $u$ (`k_hops=1`) and the $L = 3$ lookback steps $\{t - 2, t - 1, t\}$ (`l_lookback=3`), at horizon $H = 1$ (`horizon_h=1`). The loss summand is the squared residual $s_{u,t} = (Y_{u,t+H} - \hat{Y}_{u,t+H})^2$ (`metric="mse"`), and the target $\theta(D) = \mathbb{E}[s_{u,t}]$ is computed analytically (`compute_exact_theta`).
- **Design**: A 200-node ring (`graph_kind="ring"`, `num_nodes=200`, configs 1–6) or a $20 \times 20$ 4-connected lattice (`graph_kind="grid_2d"`, `num_nodes=400`, config 7) over $T = 500$ time steps (`num_times=500`), giving $T_{\text{eval}} = T - L - H + 1 = 497$ evaluated time steps per node and $n = N_s(T - L - H + 1)$ evaluation items ($99{,}400$ on the ring, $198{,}800$ on the lattice). - **Declaration and interval**: `RangeDeclaration(k_hops=1, lookback=3, horizon=1, data_range_s=decl_r_s, data_range_t=decl_r_t, gamma=decl_gamma)`, `m_item = 16.0` (for Gaussian residuals, true $M = 3 \le 16.0$), `level = 0.95` ($\alpha = 0.05$). Candidate partitions (`range_candidates`) and their certified Kronecker bounds (`range_kappa_fn(..., method="kronecker")`) are scored once per configuration by `spacetime.choose_partition` from the design alone; in each repetition only the field $X$ is redrawn and `cluster_bound.cluster_interval` is evaluated on the winning partition's cluster totals (`PartialClusterShard.from_summands`).

### 7.2 RMSE Coverage Under Dependence and Heavy-Tailed i.i.d. Designs

This benchmark compares textbook RMSE intervals (Student-$t$ on squared errors and percentile, BCa and Bayesian bootstraps; on dependent data also moving-block and cluster bootstraps) with the library's cluster interval on a space-time panel (Part A) and with `independent.interval.confidence_interval` on i.i.d. errors (Part B). Textbook endpoints for the MSE are square-rooted.

#### Setup

- **Part A (space-time dependence, config `ring_100k`)**: a 200-node ring over $T = 500$ steps; the field has spatial range 1, temporal range 2 (`range_generators.generate_space_time_field`, variant 1). ## 8. Edge-Dominance Guard Sweep and Diagnostic Calibration

### 8.1 Cluster-Graph Edge-Dominance Guard Sweep

This study measures the size and power of the cluster-graph edge check (`refutation.check_uncorrelated_edges`) with and without its dominance guard. With $z_c = e_c / \sqrt{n_c}$ and $y_{(c,d)} = z_c z_d$ on the cluster-graph edges, the check reports $\text{edge\_n\_eff} = \lVert y \rVert_1^2 / \lVert y \rVert_2^2$ and returns `UNTESTABLE` when it is below 10 (§9.4 of [THEORY.md](THEORY.md)). Without the guard, a few dominant clusters with residuals skewed the same way can make the test refute a true $\kappa = 1$ almost surely.

#### Setup

- **Configurations**: 29 cells, 2,000 repetitions each, test level 0.95. Every repetition runs the check twice: with the guard (`min_effective_edges=10`) and without it (`min_effective_edges=0`).
  - **S-a, heteroscedastic Gaussian null (3 cells)**: cluster scales $s_c \sim \text{LogNormal}(0, 0.5)$ and $\varepsilon_c \sim N(0, n_c s_c^2)$. Graphs: a random geometric graph ($G = 500$, mean degree 6.3, cluster sizes uniform on 25–74); a Barabási–Albert graph on 20,000 items ($m = 5$) partitioned with `partitioners.partition_graph` at target size 50 ($G = 348$); and a hub graph (random geometric, $G = 500$, mean degree 6, plus 3 hubs adjacent to 90% of clusters).
  - **S-b, sparse binary (18 cells)**: $S_c \sim \text{Binomial}(n_c, \theta)$ with $\theta \in \{0.99, 0.999\}$ and $n_c \in \{1, 10, 50\}$, on the geometric graph, the Barabási–Albert partition, and a perfect matching on $G = 1{,}000$ clusters.
  - **S-c, anchored skew (4 cells)**: perfect matching on $G = 1{,}000$ clusters with $n_c = 1$. The first $2k$ clusters are Bernoulli(0.999); the rest are fixed at 0.999, so they have zero variance at the common mean. $k \in \{3, 5, 10, 50\}$.
  - **S-d, small graphs (3 cells)**: random geometric graphs with $G \in \{10, 20, 40\}$ and mean degree 6, with the S-a Gaussian errors.
  - **S-e, correlated alternative (1 cell)**: the S-a geometric graph with $\varepsilon_c = \sqrt{n_c}\,(u_c + 0.1 \sum_{d \sim c} u_d)$, $u_c \sim N(0, 1)$; exact variance inflation 2.543.
- **`UNTESTABLE` reasons**: "guard" means `edge_n_eff < 10`. "Other" means every edge product $z_c z_d$ is zero (for example, every item is correct, so every residual is zero) or the graph has fewer than 30 distinct edges.
- **Seed**: Module constant `DEFAULT_SEED = 20260926` in `benchmark/edge_guard_sweep.py`.

### 8.2 Diagnostic and Refutation Calibration Simulations

`benchmark/theory_sims.py` is a self-contained NumPy simulation script (it does not call the library). It checks seven statements in §8 and §9 of [THEORY.md](THEORY.md). All tests are one-sided at level 0.05 (critical value 1.6449). "Fisher-$z$" is the lag-1 test of `refutation.check_uncorrelated`, $\operatorname{artanh}(r_1)\sqrt{G-4}$; "self-normalised" is $\sum_E x_c x_d / \sqrt{\sum_E (x_c x_d)^2}$ over the edges $E$ (consecutive windows for temporal checks).

- **S1, window-count autocorrelation (8 $(n, G)$ pairs, deterministic)**: counts of the equal-count assignment `(np.arange(n) * G) // n`; reports their lag-1 autocorrelation `rho` and the prediction $-f/(1-f)$ with $f = \min(\text{frac}, 1-\text{frac})$, $\text{frac} = n/G - \lfloor n/G \rfloor$.
- **S2, high-accuracy lag-1 tests (9 cells, 4,000 repetitions)**: $n = 20{,}070$ items in $G = 200$ equal-count windows; window error probability $\min\{(1-\theta)e^{b\eta_c - b^2/2}, 1\}$ with $\eta$ a stationary Gaussian AR(1) ($\rho = 0.5$) across windows, $\theta \in \{0.9, 0.99, 0.999\}$, $b \in \{0, 0.3, 0.6\}$ ($b = 0$ is the null). Tests: Fisher-$z$ on centred totals $S_c$ (`rej_raw`), Fisher-$z$ on residuals $e_c = S_c - n_c\hat\theta$ (`rej_resid`), self-normalised on $e_c$ (`rej_sn`). `rho_tilde_mean` is the mean of $\hat\theta^2 \sum_c (n_c - \bar n)^2 / \sum_c e_c^2$, the size of the count component relative to the residuals.
- **S3, unequal time buckets (4 cells, 4,000 repetitions)**: $G = 300$, $\theta = 0.9$, same error model with $b \in \{0, 0.3\}$. Count profiles: `lognorm`, $\max\{1, \operatorname{round}(e^{N(\ln 100, 1)})\}$; `trend`, $\operatorname{round}(100\,e^{t})$ for $t$ evenly spaced on $[-1, 1]$. Fisher-$z$ on centred totals (`fz_raw`), on residuals (`fz_res`), and on $z_c = e_c/\sqrt{n_c}$ (`fz_norm`); self-normalised on $e_c$ (`sn_res`) and on $z_c$ (`sn_norm`).
- **S4, cluster-graph edge test (4 cells, 4,000 repetitions)**: random geometric graph, $G = 500$, connection radius $\sqrt{6/(\pi G)}$; counts $\max\{1, \operatorname{round}(e^{N(\ln 100, 0.7)})\}$; totals $S_c = 0.3\,n_c + \sqrt{n_c}\,((I + bA)\xi)_c$ with $\xi \sim N(0, I)$, $b \in \{0, 0.03, 0.06, 0.1\}$. Reports the exact variance inflation `vif` $= \mathbf 1^\top \Sigma \mathbf 1 / \operatorname{tr}\Sigma$, its edge-local version `vif_w` $= 1 + \sum_{c \sim d} \Sigma_{cd} / \operatorname{tr}\Sigma$, the mean of the estimator $\hat\kappa_W = 1 + 2\sum_E e_c e_d / \sum_c e_c^2$ (`kappa_w`), and the self-normalised edge test on $e_c$ (`rej_e`) and on $z_c$ (`rej_z`).
- **S5, $R^2$ bound from two unpaired sketches (4 cells, 2,000 repetitions)**: $G$ clusters of $m$ items; labels with total variance 1 and intra-cluster correlation `icc_y`, residuals with mean square 0.2 and intra-cluster correlation `icc_e`, so the target is $R^2 = 0.8$. The interval combines Cantelli-type bounds for the two sketches with $M = 3$, $\kappa = 1$, $\alpha = 0.05$ (computed inline). Reports misses below and above (`miss_lo`, `miss_hi`), median endpoints (`med_lo`, `med_hi`), and the mean sample $R^2$ (`emp_r2`).
- **S6, uncorrelated but dependent totals (3 cells, 4,000 repetitions)**: $\varepsilon_c = \xi_c \xi_{c-1}$ with $\xi$ i.i.d. $N(0, 1)$, so lag-1 correlation is zero but the sequence is dependent; $G \in \{100, 400, 1{,}600\}$. Fisher-$z$ (`fz`) vs. self-normalised on the centred sequence (`sn`).
- **S7, hub-heavy graphs under the null (5 cells, 4,000 repetitions)**: centred i.i.d. $N(0, 1)$ cluster residuals; self-normalised edge test (`rej`). Graphs: Barabási–Albert with $m = 2$ ($G = 500$ and $2{,}000$); "hubs $k \times p$": every vertex linked to 2 random vertices, plus $k$ hubs each adjacent to every other vertex with probability $p$.

## 9. Cluster-Granularity Sweep

This study measures how the cluster-totals bound (`cluster_bound.cluster_interval` with `kappa_cluster=1.0`) behaves when the asserted cluster independence is wrong, and how the moment-bound check reacts to under-declared $M$. It supports the robustness margin $\kappa^*$ and the granularity rule in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) §5.4.

#### Setup

- **Data**: $n = 100{,}000$ standard Gaussian errors in equicorrelated blocks of size $m_{\text{true}}$ (`block_size`) with within-block correlation $\rho$; metric `mse`, true value $1$. The summand $e^2$ has $M = 3$.
- **Estimator**: clusters of `cluster_size` consecutive items, either aligned to block boundaries or offset by half a block. For `cluster_size = 1` the benchmark calls the independent-sample interval with the exact `effective_n` (reference route D0). For `cluster_size > 1` it calls `cluster_interval(..., kappa_cluster=1.0)` (route D1). That assertion is true only when clusters contain whole aligned blocks.
- **$\kappa_{\text{true}}$ and $\kappa^*$**: $\kappa_{\text{true}}$ is the maximum absolute row sum of the cluster-total correlation matrix (exact for aligned, evenly dividing grids; a heuristic otherwise). $\kappa^* = \dfrac{(M_d - 1)(1 - \alpha')/\alpha'}{(M_c - 1)\, z_{1-\alpha'}^2}$ with $\alpha' = \alpha/2$ and the exact cluster-level $M_c$ is a normal-approximation margin: the multiple by which the asserted $\kappa = 1$ may be wrong before coverage breaks. It is a diagnostic only, and it applies to route D1; route D0 is given the exact `effective_n` and asserts nothing about $\kappa$.
- **Suites**:
  1. `granularity`: $m_{\text{true}} \in \{10, 50, 250\}$ × $\rho \in \{0.3, 0.8\}$ × `cluster_size` $\in \{1, 5, 25, 250, 500\}$ × aligned (plus offset where `cluster_size` $\ge m_{\text{true}}$) × $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.05, 0.01\}$; 132 configurations, $M_d = 4$, 2,000 repetitions each.
  2. `calibration`: $m_{\text{true}} \in \{80, 100, 125, 160, 200, 250\}$, $\rho = 0.8$, `cluster_size = 5`, aligned, all three $\alpha$; 18 configurations, $M_d = 4$, 5,000 repetitions each.
  3. `refutation`: $m_{\text{true}} = 50$, $\rho \in \{0.3, 0.8\}$, `cluster_size` $\in \{1, 5, 25\}$, aligned, $\alpha = 0.05$, $M_d \in \{1.2, 1.5, 2.0, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 3.0, 4.0\}$; 48 configurations, 2,000 repetitions each.
- **Seed**: Module constant `DEFAULT_SEED = 20260923` in `benchmark/cluster_sweep.py`; per-configuration seeds are derived deterministically from it.

#### Commands

```bash
bazel run -c opt //dgf/src/stats/benchmark:cluster_sweep -- --suite=granularity --mode=full
bazel run -c opt //dgf/src/stats/benchmark:cluster_sweep -- --suite=calibration --mode=full
bazel run -c opt //dgf/src/stats/benchmark:cluster_sweep -- --suite=refutation --mode=full
```
